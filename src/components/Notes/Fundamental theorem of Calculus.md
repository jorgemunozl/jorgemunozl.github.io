>[!theorem]
Let $f:I\to \mathbb{R}$ continuous in $I$, another function $F:I\to \mathbb{R}$ are equivalent: [^1]
1. $F$ is a indefinite integral of $f$, this is $\exists a\in I$ such that:

$$
F(x)=F(a)+\int_{a}^{x}f(t)dt,\forall x\in I
$$

2. $F$ is the the primitive from $f$, this is $F'(x)=f(x)$ for all $x\in I$.

This theorem relates [[First derivative on one dimension]] with the integral. 
This is proved that all continuous function has a primitive. (exist F)  also is proved that if $F$ es  $C^{1}$ class first derivative continuous then it could be written like the sum of a constant and the integral of his derivative.
