Let $S$ be a set with $n$ elements. A permutation is a [[Bijective]] [[Map like the generalization of a function]] $\sigma:S\to S$. Thus to each element we associate to another element.

>[!example]
>$S=\{ 1,2,3 \}$
$\sigma(1,2,3)=(2,3,1)$ so ,$\sigma(1)=2,\sigma(2)=3,\sigma(3)=1$.
>

We often use the two line notation:
$$
\pi=\begin{pmatrix}
1&2&3 \\
2&3&1
\end{pmatrix}
$$
Or the representation of trough cycles.
$$
(1 \ 2  \ 3)
$$
A **transposition** is a unique permutation: So all cycle could be written in like a odd or even number of transposition ([[Parity of a Permutation]])
