When you have a [[Capacitors]], and use the [[Ampere Law]], there are two surfaces that you can consider, one which is covering a plaque and another which is covering the wire where one plaque is connected.

The one which is not covering give you the integral equals zero, whereas the other is different from zero, what's happening here? The law doesn't work.

How we fix that, introducing a new term!

$$
\oint \vec{B}\cdot \vec{\ell}=\mu_{0} (I_{\text{int}}+\varepsilon_{0}\frac{d}{dt} \phi_{E})
$$
- The term  introduced is called **Displacement** [[Current Intensity]].
- But this is actually a ton, is telling us that a change on the [[Electric Field]] is producing a change in the [[Magnetic Field]].
- But for that matter $I_{\text{int}}$ should vary also. Which means that if you know how the [[charge on a conductor]] vary you know the modulu of $\vec{B}$. Because the orientation is simply the one which actually produce a wire (intensity current).
