The circular movement refers to an object rotating respect at axis in two dimensions. And the beauty of this is that it could be construct and analogous to the [[Curved movement]] trough [[Newton Second Law]]. Using the [[Moment of Inertia]] and [[Angular Momentum]].

In this case use the [[Polar Coordinate System]] make things very easy to analyse.

$$
\vec{x}=(R\cos(\omega t+\phi),R\sin(\omega t+\phi))
$$

Where $\omega$ is the _angular velocity_ and $\phi$ _offset angle_.

Supposing that $\omega$ is constant (don't depends of time or any other variable, chain rule for that case) we have.

$$
\vec{v}=(R\omega.-\sin(\omega t+\phi),R\omega.\cos(\omega t+\phi))
$$

Thus also we can obtain the _acceleration_.

We can't forget that that _angular velocity_ is a vector, perpendicular to the plane of rotation,  and it's direction is obtained using that left hand rule, and it's is pretty related to the _velocity_.

$$
\vec{v}=\vec{\omega}\times\vec{r}
$$
