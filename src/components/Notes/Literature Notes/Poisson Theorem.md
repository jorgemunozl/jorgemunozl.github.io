Give a vector $A$ in a rotating [[Reference Frame, and inertial frame|reference frame]] with angular velocity $w$ its time derivative in a inertial _frame_ $(S)$ and in the rotating frame $(S´)$ are related by:

$$\left( 
\frac{d\mathbf{A}}{dt} \right)_{inertial}=\left( \frac{d\mathbf{A}}{dt} \right)_{rotating}+w \times \mathbf{A}
$$

- So from here can we say $\vec{v}=\vec{\omega}\times \vec{r}$. Yeah if you choose that the inertial and the rotating system are the same.
- This theorem explain the [[Coriolis effect]].
- So here we could what important is the axis of rotation.
