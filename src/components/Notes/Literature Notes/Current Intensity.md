>[!definition]
>The current intensity measure the quantity of charge that flows trough for a unit of time on a conductor. The units that we appoint to this are the **Ampere** in honor to the French Marie Ampere.
- Can be derived from first principles:
- Average current:
$$ I_{avg}=\frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta t} $$
- Instantaneous current: [[Microscope model current intensity]]
$$ I\equiv \frac{dQ}{dt} $$
- This induces a vector $(\vec{J})$ called [[Density Current]].

$E$ [[Electric Field]], and $\sigma$ is known as **conductivity**. 

$$
\Delta V=E\ell=\frac{J}{\sigma}\ell=I\left( \frac{\ell}{A\sigma} \right) 
$$
Then [[Resistors]] are defined as:
$$
R=\frac{\ell}{A\sigma }
$$

And defining the **resistivity** as the inverse of the **conductivity**
$$
\rho=\frac{1}{\sigma}
$$ 
You obtain:
$$
R=\rho\frac{ \ell}{A}
$$


Now there exist the conservation of: [[Crawl Speed]] [[Electric Power Potency]] which is modeled by a differential equation.
