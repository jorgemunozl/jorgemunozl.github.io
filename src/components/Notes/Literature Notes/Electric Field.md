Definition:
$$
\vec{E}\equiv\frac{\vec{F}}{q_{0}}
$$
For a punctual charge we have:
$$
\vec{E}=k_{e}\frac{q}{r^{2}}\vec{r}
$$
For many charges we simply sum:
$$
\vec{E}=k_{e}\sum_{i}\frac{q_{i}}{r_{i}^{2}}\hat{r}_{i}
$$


And this inevitable make us to thing on [[field lines]]


The pretty relies on when we talk of continuous charge because we need to talk of differential and use calculus.

$$
\vec{E}=k_{e}\lim_{ \Delta q_{i} \to 0 }\sum_{i}\frac{\Delta q_{i}}{r_{i}^{2}}\hat{r}_{i}=k_{e}\int \frac{dq}{r^{2}}\hat{r}
$$
Then we need to replace $dq$ with density , area, length.
