We say that a bound [[Real function]] $f$ is integrable if the [[Upper and lower integral]] are the same. To the common value we say [[integral]] (**Riemann**). (Darboux approach)

Are equivalent : $f$ is integrable, $\forall\varepsilon>0,\exists P,Q|S(f,Q)-s(f,P)<\varepsilon$, $S(f,P)-s(f,P)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}(t_{i}-t_{i-1})<\varepsilon$

And more practical way to see it's like the area under the curve. $f:[a,b]\to \mathbb{R}$.

$$
\lim_{ n \to \infty } \sum_{i=1}^{n} f\left( x_{i}^{*} \right) \Delta x,x_{i}=a+i\Delta x,\Delta x=\frac{b-a}{n}$$

The _Riemann Integral_ is the sum when taking that limits exist, is independent form the choose of $x_{i}^{*}$.

The most easy way to calculate an integral is using the [[Fundamental theorem of Calculus]]
