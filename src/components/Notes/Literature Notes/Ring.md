Another [[Algebraic Structure]] is the ring. Unlike a group, which has only one operator, a ring has two operators that must satisfy certain properties. 
For a structure to be called a ring, the first operator must form an [[Abelian group]] with the set, and the second must distribute over the first.

>[!definition] Definition of a Ring
> Let $(G,\chi$,$\eta)$ be a ring. Then:
> $(G,\chi)$ is an [[Abelian group]].
> $\eta$ is associative.
>$\eta$ distributes over $\chi$. (Like the product of a lifetime) [^1]

Exist two special kind of rings.
1. _Unital Ring_, Here exists the _neuter element_ of the "product". ($\mathbb{1} \neq \mathbb{0}$)
2. _Ring with division_ .- Here exist the _inverse element_ for "product", (for all element of the set except $\mathbb{0}$
It's important to say that in both cases we exclude the _neuter element_ of the addition of the set where the two definition could be applied.
