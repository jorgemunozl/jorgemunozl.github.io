>[!theorem]
>Let $S$ be a regular surface orientable , parametrizada by $\vec{r}\in C^{2}$.
>Let $\vec{F} : \mathbb{R}^{3}\to \mathbb{R}^{3}$ be a [[Vector field]]  define over the open set $S\subset U$ then the follow is [^1] true:
>$$
\oint \limits_{\partial S  } \vec{F} \cdot d\ \vec{\alpha}=\iint \limits_{S} \nabla \times   \vec{F} \cdot d \vec{s}$$

- Realize that the first part of the equations is the [[Vector line integral|line integral]] and the second part is a [[Surface Integral (second specie)|surface integral]] of the [[Curl]] of $\vec{F}$.
- The first part is over the frontier of the surface.
- This is the case when the **vector field** acts on the $\mathbb{R}^{3}$ space but the theorem could expressed in $\mathbb{R}^{n}$. But that goes beyond our topic.
- Is necessary to understand that this is like the generalization from the [[Fundamental theorem of Calculus]].
- For the second dimension we have the [[Green Theorem]]. 
[^1]: [[Analisis Matematico III Calculo Vectorial]] pp. 818
