>[!theorem]
> Let  be $V\subset \mathbb{R}^{2}$ a open region, $D\subset V$ a [[Simply connected region]]  which frontier is $\partial D$  (this should be a [[Path|regular curve]] and simple, inyective). 
> Let be $f=(P,Q)$ a [[Vector field]], with  $P,Q,\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x},\frac{\partial P}{\partial y }$ [[Continuity (Many Dimension)|continuous ]] [[Scalar function|scalar functions]] from $\mathbb{R}^{2}\to \mathbb{R}$, then [^1].
>$$
\oint_{\partial D} Pdx +Qdy =\iint \limits_{D}(\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} -\frac{\partial P}{\partial y})dxdy$$

- Realize that the second is a [[Double Integral]] and the first is a [[Vector line integral]].
- The orientation of the curve should be positive (**counterclockwise**). If that were not the case then the equation has a negative sign.
- Now why the argument of the second part has a similar form? 

[^1]: [[Analisis Matematico III Calculo Vectorial]] pp. 755
