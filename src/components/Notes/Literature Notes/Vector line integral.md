>[!definition]
>Give it a [[Vector field]] $\vec{F}$ defined over $D$ and a [[Path|regular curve]] (seccional is also good , we could break it) $\vec{\alpha}$, we define the **line integral of second specie** as [^1]: 
>$$\int_{C}\vec{F}\cdot d\vec{\alpha}=\int_{a}^{b}\vec{F}(\vec{\alpha}(t))\cdot\alpha'(t)dt$$
>Where the point $\cdot$ is the [[Euclid Norm, product|dot product]].

- Also known as **vector line integral**.
- And if is [[exact one differential form]] then the integral is zero, we could affirm that is conservative?, don't forget that regions have to be nice, if that happen yes! Is like a practical test, but how you prove it? Well using the [[theorem conservative force]], and this theorem of course.
