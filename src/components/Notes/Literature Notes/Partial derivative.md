>[!definition]
>How one can define the derivative of a [[Scalar function]] as [^1]:
$$ D_{\mathbf{u}}f(\mathrm{x})=\lim_{ h \to 0 }\frac{f(\mathrm{x}+h\mathbf{u})-f(\mathrm{x})}{h}$$ 


- A beauty theorem says if $f$ is differentiable on $\mathrm{x}$, then then the directional derivative is $D_{\mathbf{u}}f(\mathrm{x})=Df(\mathrm{x})\cdot \mathbf{u}$, the not rigorous prove is [[partial.svg#D|this.]]


Give it a [[Scalar function]], and $\mathbf{u}_{k}$ is the vector with component $k$ th and the others zero. The partial **derivative** is defined with by $D_{\mathbf{u}_{k}}f$.
Remember $\mathbf{u}$ is a _unitary vector_.
