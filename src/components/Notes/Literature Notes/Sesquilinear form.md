>[!definition]
>Let $V$ and $\mathcal{W}$ be $\mathbb{C}$ [[Vector Space]], a [[Map like the generalization of a function|map]] $s:V\times \mathcal{W}\to \mathbb{C}$ is a called a sesquilinear form on $V\times \mathcal{W}$ when.

$$
\begin{align}
1)s(v+v,w) & =s(v,w)+s(v2,w) \\
2)s(\lambda v,w) & =\lambda s(v,w) \\
3)s(v,w_{1}+w_{2}) & =s(v,w_{1})+s(v,w_{2}) \\
4)s(v,\lambda w) & =\overline{\lambda}s(v,w)
\end{align}
$$

- **Sesquilinearity** for $\mathbb{C}$ is lineal for the first argument and **semi linear** for the second argument. (conjugate in the second argument).

>With this we can assure that the **Norm** would be a **real value**. Is just that.
