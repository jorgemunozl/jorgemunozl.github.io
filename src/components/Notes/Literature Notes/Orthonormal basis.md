>[!definition]
>A set of vectors (for us is important that it would be [[Basis Of A Space Vector (Hamel)|basis]]) is **orthonormal** if: [^1]
>1. The vectors are [[Orthogonality, Complement Idea|orthogonal]] this is, the [[Dot Product, why define it like that?|scalar product]] is zero: $\langle u_{i},u_{j} \rangle=0$ for $i\neq j$ .
>2. Each vector has unit [[Norm]]: $\lVert u_{i} \rVert=1$

- One important example is the [[Fourier Orthogonal Basis]].
- [[Gram Schimdt Procedure|How we obtain them]]? From a [[Linearly independent]] set. Computationally talking what if the vectors are narrowly l.d.
- I mean is natural to use them, use them is a easy way to work with them. Is like the follow step from the Cartesian Space.
