>[!definition]
>Two [[Euclid Vector Space]] are **isomorphism** or **unitary** **equivalents**, $\mathcal{E}_{1}=\mathcal{E}_{2}$, if exist a [[Linear Map]] $\varphi:\mathcal{E_{1}}\to \mathcal{E}_{2}$ [[Bijective]] in such a way:
>$$ \forall f,g\in \mathcal{E}_{1}, (f,g)_{1}=(\varphi(f),\varphi(g))_{2} $$

- To the map $\varphi$ we call [[Unitary Operator|unitary transformation]] from $\mathcal{E}_{1}$ over $\mathcal{E}_{2}$. 
- In general (not necessarily **bijective**) a map that preserves the [[Inner product]] we call a **isometry** [^1].
- But the interesting is that only we ask the surjectivity the injectivity is inside the definition (Prove).

[^1]: [[Linear Algebra Done Right]] pp. 259
