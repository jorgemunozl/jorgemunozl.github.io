We say that $f$ is differentiable on t, (inner point of $I$) if exist a [[Linear Map]] $L :\mathbb{R}\to \mathbb{R}^{n}$ such that: $(h,h+t\in I)$
$$f (t+ h) = f(t) + Lh + r h$$
Where $\lim_{ h \to 0 }\frac{u(h)}{h}=0$, $L$ is called [[derivatives|derivative]] or differential and is notated as $Df(t)$.

**Theorem**: $f$ is differentiable on $t\in I$ $\iff$ $f$ have tangent on $f(t)$
$$
\lim_{ h\to 0 }\frac{1}{h}[f(x+h)-f(x)]-l =0
$$
And of course if $f$ is differentiable on $t\in I$ then $f$ is continuous on $t$.
