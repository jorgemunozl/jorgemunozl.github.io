>The [[resistance on a fluid|resistance]] that offer a [[An introduction to fluids]] is proportional to the velocity that it has. This is:
>$$ \vec{F_{r}}\propto \vec{\nu}$$

From this relation **Stokes** could derive the follow relation for a sphere inside a **fluid**, being more exactly he found the constant in between: 

>Let $R$ be the radius of the sphere, $\eta$ the [[Coefficient viscosity]], then the force owe to the resistance is: 
$$\vec{F}_v=-6 \pi R \eta  \vec{v}
$$

Obtain the [[terminal velocity - friction]] under this situation is relatively easy, this is (Supposing initial velocity zero) :
$$V_{Terminal}=\frac{2}{9} \frac{g R^2 (\rho_{Esfera}-\rho_{Liquido})}{\eta}$$
