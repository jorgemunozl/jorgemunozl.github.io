The range-image of [[Linear Map]] is a set of vectors that belongs to _the arrival set_. This vectors are characterized because are the result of mapping any vector of the _starting set_. This is.
Let $T:V\to W$ be a linear transformation, then its range is: [^1]
$$ Ran(T)=\{ g \in W | g=T(v), v \in V\} $$
- Analogously to the [[Kernel of Linear Map|kernel]], it's necessary have a way to represent to the the _arrival set_ in such a way that is related to the the **Linear Map**. (This is more directly and obvious). 
- Is clear that this is also a [[Sub Vector Space]].
- So what about the [[Dimension of a Vector space|Dimension]], it has a ton of sense that is the number of [[Linearly independent]] vectors on the [[Matrix representation of map]].

[^1]: [[Linear Algebra Done Right]] pp. 75
