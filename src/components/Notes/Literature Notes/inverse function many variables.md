>[!theorem]
>Let $F$ be a [[Vector Function]] which is derivable at a point $\mathrm{a}$ and the [[Jacobian Matrix]] is [[Invertible Matrix|invertible]] then the follow is truth. [^1]
> - Exist two open where the function is [[Bijective]].
> - Where is **bijective** then the [[Function Inverse]] is continuously differentiable.
> - And for each value $\mathrm{y}$ on the **inverse** function the derivative can be obtain like:
>$$D(f^{-1})(y)=[Df(f^{-1}(y))]^{-1}$$

- This is the **Jacobian matrix** of the inverse function is the inverse of the **Jacobian matrix**.(Beauty, those words recall to something)
- Now this is another demonstration of functions that are not well defined but you can obtain the derivative of them in such a way [[Implicit Derivatives]]. 

[^1]: [[Multivariable Calculus Lectures]] pp. 56
