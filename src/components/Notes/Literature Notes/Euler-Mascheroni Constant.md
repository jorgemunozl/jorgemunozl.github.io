Since the [[Harmonic Series]] doesn't converges we have the follow option.
>[!definition]
>This constant is defined as the follow **limit**:[^1]
$$\gamma=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow \infty}[ \sum_{k=1}^{n}(\frac{1}{n})-\ln (n) ]$$
- One could give in its integral form: $\gamma=-\int_{1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{x}-\frac{1}{\lfloor x\rfloor } \, dx$
- Until know nobody knows if it is a rational or irrational. (Hard to say)
 - How we know that this limit converges?
- We know that the harmonica series do not converges, but the most of the time we work to with this sequence.
- How we can use this sequence? Here it's necessary to introduce this constant.
- Consequently this constant appears in various fields of mathematics and physics, it is necessary to understand it in detail to fully benefit from its applications.

> Euler, p.55
 > Euler had the idea of compare the growth of this divergent series with the _ln(n)_.

[^1]: [[Mathematical Methods for Physicist]] pp. 343
