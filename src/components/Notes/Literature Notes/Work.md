>[!definition]
>Let $\vec{F}$ be a [[Newton Second Law|force]] applied to a particle undergoing a displacement $d\mathbf{r}$. The **differential work** done is: [^1]
>$$dW=\vec{F}\cdot d\mathbf{\vec{r}}$$

- It have units **Newton meter**.
- When the particle moves from $A$ to $B$, a [[Path]] $\mathcal{C}$ that pass for those points then $W=\int_{\mathcal{C}} \vec{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}$ , a [[Vector line integral|line integral]]!
- If $\vec{F}$ and $d\mathbf{r}$ are parallel in all the trajectory then : $W=\int_{a}^{b}Fdr$.
- For what reason we would define this thing. [[work idea physics|Measure of tiredness]].
- Here $d\mathbf{r}$ could be caused by the force itself or another force? I guess that depends on the physical case.
