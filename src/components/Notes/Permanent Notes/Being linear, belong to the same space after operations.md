Explain the [[Linear Map|linearity]] linearity just by saying the homogeneity and additive is so rigid. 

The first notion that we have with the word linear in math, is the linear function (basically a line in the two dimensional space).

The essence is that if we __add__ two vectors that __belong__ to this line, the answer also belong to the line, if we __scale__ a  vector the result is going to __belong__ to the line.  

Nothing comes out of there, play with these operations how much you want, the structure of the line is [[Preserve the structure - Homomorphism meaning|preserved]], if the transformation were not linear this could be bend or distort, like a quadratic function. 

But of course we always plays for the generalization in math (in physics we care about the specific case), for that reason we apply this concept to more complex [[Vector Space]] that we can't visualize.
